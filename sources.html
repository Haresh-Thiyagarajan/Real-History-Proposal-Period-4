How did the 1993 Jack in the Box E. coli outbreak, which started with children who were sick in Washington state, cause the government to change how ground beef is regulated, and what changed in how fast food is made and inspected after this incident?

Chapter 05 · Literature review

Historians and sources

Researchers tell different stories about whether 1993 began the change, accelerated it, or marked one stage in a longer process.

LITERATURE REVIEW

Four researchers, four useful limits

Each summary is paraphrased, not quoted. The perspective and reach of each source remain visible.

Juska, Arunas, et al. “Manufacturing Bacteriological Contamination Outbreaks in Industrialized Meat Production Systems: The Case of E. coli O157:H7.” Agriculture and Human Values, vol. 20, no. 1, Mar. 2003, pp. 3–19.
Paraphrased, not quoted
Open source ↗

Juska et al., Agriculture and Human Values, 2003

Main argument
Industry concentration since the 1980s made meat production vulnerable to fast-spreading outbreaks, so 1993 was a product of the system. They also ask whether HACCP really works.

How it helps
Tests whether 1993 was a surprise or was bound to happen.

Perspective / bias
Sociologists writing for food and farming scholars, and openly critical of the meat industry.

What it cannot establish
It cannot establish what happened in Washington specifically, or anything after 2003.

Dunn, Elizabeth. “Escherichia coli, Corporate Discipline and the Failure of the Sewer State.” Space and Polity, vol. 11, no. 1, Apr. 2007, pp. 35–53.
Paraphrased, not quoted
Open source ↗

Dunn, Space and Polity, 2007

Main argument
HACCP replaced inspectors checking every carcass with companies policing themselves, a shift she ties to the Jack in the Box outbreak and judges a failure.

How it helps
Explains what changed in how meat is inspected.

Perspective / bias
A geographer making a theoretical argument to other academics.

What it cannot establish
It says little about 1993 itself, and is an argument rather than a timeline.

Juska, Arunas, et al. “Negotiating Bacteriological Meat Contamination Standards in the US: The Case of E. coli O157:H7.” Sociologia Ruralis, vol. 40, no. 2, Apr. 2000, pp. 249–71.
Paraphrased, not quoted
Open source ↗

Juska et al., Sociologia Ruralis, 2000

Main argument
Meat-safety standards were negotiated in stages: before 1982, after 1982, after 1993, after 1996. Each was shaped by money, politics and science, not one event.

How it helps
The best tool for testing whether the outbreak was the main cause or one cause.

Perspective / bias
Sociologists focused on how standards are bargained over by interest groups.

What it cannot establish
It cannot weigh how much each force mattered in exact terms.

Murano, Elsa A., et al. “The Outbreak That Changed Meat and Poultry Inspection Systems Worldwide.” Animal Frontiers, vol. 8, no. 4, 2018, pp. 4–8.
Paraphrased, not quoted
Open source ↗

Murano, Cross & Riggs, Animal Frontiers, 2018

Main argument
The outbreak changed meat and poultry inspection worldwide by accelerating mandatory HACCP and risk-based inspection, work that had begun in 1992.

How it helps
An inside view of how officials reacted.

Perspective / bias
Written by former senior USDA food-safety officials, with an interest in presenting the reforms as a success.

What it cannot establish
It cannot independently prove its own “worldwide” claim; that needs other countries' sources.

WHERE SOURCES DISAGREE

The totals and the causal story do not line up neatly

How many people were sick?

CDC, MMWR, 16 Apr. 1993

583 cases meeting the case definitions across four states (477 + 14 + 34 + 58), and “more than 500” laboratory-confirmed.

Rangel et al., Emerging Infectious Diseases, 2005

More than 700 people ill (paraphrased).

Murano, Cross & Riggs, Animal Frontiers, 2018

600 or more illnesses.

The totals differ because each source counts differently (case definitions, laboratory confirmation, later additions). None of them is simply wrong.

Was the outbreak the cause or the accelerator?

Dunn, Space and Polity, 2007

Connects the start of the HACCP system to the outbreak.

Murano, Cross & Riggs, Animal Frontiers, 2018

HACCP work began in 1992; the outbreak accelerated it.

Juska et al., Sociologia Ruralis, 2000

One stage among several, shaped by money, politics and science.

This disagreement is the investigation.

United States, Department of Agriculture, Food Safety and Inspection Service. “Mandatory Safe Handling Statements on Labeling of Raw Meat and Poultry Products.” Federal Register, vol. 59, no. 59, 28 Mar. 1994, p. 14528.

Texas Food Industry Association v. Espy, 870 F. Supp. 143

Texas Food Industry Association v. Espy. 870 F. Supp. 143. United States District Court, Western District of Texas, 13 Dec. 1994.

Why it matters: Industry groups sued to stop USDA testing raw ground beef for E. coli O157:H7; the court refused to block the program.

Supports: That treating O157:H7 as an adulterant was a real legal change, and that industry contested it.
